# pyntpot

![A hand-painted map of Keswick and Derwentwater: green and cream washes for the land, blue strokes for the rivers Derwent and Greta, orange roads and a pink route looping through the town, with the names written by hand](images/hero.webp)

*Painted by `examples/place_card.py` (size 1200).*

pyntpot paints watercolour and pen-and-ink pictures in Python the way a painter works:
paper first, then washes, brushes, the pen, and the names last. Maps are its first use
and not the whole of it. Choose the route that fits what you want to do.

## Choose a route

- **Learn by painting.** The [tutorials](tutorials/index.md) take you from a sheet of
  paper to a route map in order, and then to pictures that are not maps.
- **Get a job done.** The [how-to guides](how-to/index.md) each solve one task: write a
  theme, map from the command line, work offline, credit the data.
- **Look something up.** The [reference](reference/index.md) lists the command line,
  every style field, the cache and the manifest, and the pigment and brush sheets.
- **Understand why.** The [explanation](explanation/index.md) pages describe how the wash
  works, the hand and the nib, and the data's licences.

Every picture in these docs is in the [gallery](gallery.md), with the script that paints
it. The [glossary](reference/glossary.md) gives the name of each thing in the studio.
